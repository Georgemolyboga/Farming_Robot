# Below is a Bill of Materials (BOM) and reasoning document for the design of a closed-loop DC motor control subsystem. This document outlines the functional and non-functional requirements, evaluates component options, and provides trade-off analyses to guide the selection of hardware components.

## Functional Requirements (FRs)

| ID | Description | Characteristic to look for | Ranges |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR-1 | Each wheel is automatically pushed toward its target speed: measure, compare, correct (PI loop). | **No-load speed at your real battery voltage** | Keep the top target ≤ ~70 % of no-load RPM. 90 RPM target → motor needs ≥ ~130 RPM (FIT0450: 160 RPM @ 6 V → ~112 RPM usable). |
| FR-2 | Count encoder pulses to know how far and in which direction each wheel turned, without glitches when the counter rolls over. | **Counts per wheel revolution after decoding (N)**, plus timer width (16- vs 32-bit) | N ≥ 360 (≤ 1° per count). N = motor-shaft counts × gear ratio. Check whether the vendor's figure is already decoded (×1, ×2 or ×4). |
| FR-3 | The state machine only accepts a new "go" after the wheels are really stopped (< 3 RPM for 300 ms). | **Encoder resolution at very low speed** | At 3 RPM and N = 360 you get ~5.4 counts in 300 ms. With N < ~100 you get < 2 counts, so rest can't be judged reliably. |
| FR-4 | Show target vs. measured RPM and angle on a small screen. | **Controller's rated SPI write clock and display RAM size** | 240×240×16 bit = 115 kB per full frame (320×240 = 154 kB). One 240×240 frame at 10 MHz ≈ 92 ms. |
| FR-5 | A physical stop button cuts motor power in hardware, even if the software has crashed. | **Driver Enable/Standby pin (STBY, nSLEEP, D1/D2) with default-off pull-down, plus E-stop contact type (NC)** | Drivers without an enable pin (e.g. MDD10A) need an external power cut or PWM gate. |
| FR-6 | Knobs/switches can drive the rover if Bluetooth doesn't work. | **Free ADC-capable and GPIO pins left after encoders, PWM, SPI, UART and E-stop** | CJ1 needs ~16–19 pins in total. A Nucleo-64 has plenty. |

### Non-Functional Requirements

| ID | Description | Characteristic to look for | Ranges |
|---|---|---|---|
| NFR-1 | The control loop must tick every 20 ms (±2 ms), finish in ≤ 2 ms and never be late. | **Core clock + hardware FPU + interrupt latency/priorities** | At 100 MHz, 2 ms = 200,000 clock cycles. Two PI loops need only a small fraction. Display and BLE must never block the interrupt. |
| NFR-2 | Angle within 10°, speed within ±5 RPM or ±10 %. | **Gearbox backlash (degrees at the output) and N** | Resolution = 360/N per count. Speed resolution = 60/(N × window): N = 360 gives 0.83 RPM at 200 ms but 8.3 RPM at 20 ms, which breaks the ±5 RPM limit. A motor-shaft encoder does not see gearbox backlash. |
| NFR-3 | Screen refreshes ≥ 10 times/s with data ≤ 200 ms old, without slowing the motors. | **SPI clock (and DMA support) vs. frame size** | A full 320×240 frame at the ILI9341's rated 10 MHz takes ~123 ms (~8 fps), which is too slow. Use partial redraws or a faster clock. |
| NFR-4 | If the program hangs, a watchdog resets the system within 1 s and the motors stay off during reboot. | **Independent watchdog on its own oscillator, and GPIO state during reset** | Pins float at reset, so add an external pull-down on the driver enable. |
| NFR-5 | A phone command within 3 m is acknowledged in < 1 s. | **BLE TX power / RX sensitivity (dBm) and connection interval** | RN4870/71: 0 dBm / −90 dBm, which is plenty for 3 m. Delay comes from the connection interval (tens of ms). |
| NFR-6 | Motor noise and current spikes must not reset the electronics (MCU, Pi). | **Motor stall current (A) vs. battery maximum discharge current and regulator rating** | FIT0450 pair stalled = 2 × 2.8 A = 5.6 A. Use a separate regulated branch for the Pi and the logic, with a bulk capacitor near the driver. |

### CJ1 fit summary (AI generated and to be discussed)
- **Board:** F411RE remains the best fit (named in the proposal, cheap, 5 encoder-capable timers, only 2 needed). The 4-motor variant in this file needs 4 encoder timers, which F411RE, F446RE, G431RB and C562RE all offer.
- **Motors:** FIT0450 or a 6 V JGB37-520 fit the €60 budget. The Pololu 25D is over budget. The NEMA 17 does not fit.
- **Driver:** TB6612FNG or DRV8833 for stalls up to ~1.5–2 A. MC33926 if you want to survive 2.8 A stall and get dedicated disable pins. Avoid the L298N (voltage drop) and the MDD10A (no enable pin, no reverse protection).
- **BLE:** RN4870/71 (via breakout) or Bluefruit UART Friend. Prove the real phone app in week 4 either way.
